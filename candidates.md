# Candidates — Tell a Whole Story in 32 Characters

Competition: u-xvader-tell-a-whole-story-in-32-characters-f86dd978
Brief (in order): 1. a Wi-Fi network name ≤32 chars that reads as a joke and hints
at a story · 2. joke lands on first read, clear twist/punchline · 3. whole story:
situation + character + something that happened · 4. fresh (avoid stock Wi-Fi jokes
or turn one into something new) · 5. tight, clean, easy to say out loud.

Ranking is MANUAL against the brief in its own order + pairwise against the
strongest rival shapes. No Gemini judge, no NVIDIA judge, no judge API/proxy
tournament (standing rule from Rev, 2026-11-24).

Character counts verified by script (see the count check in this session).

## The candidates

1. `NoWiFiSinceTheDivorce` — 21 chars
   Rationale: the whole divorce in four words — the Wi-Fi died with the marriage
   and never came back. Situation (no Wi-Fi), character (the divorcee), event
   (the divorce, the ex took everything). Punchline IS the premise.
2. `MyExChangedTheWiFiPassword` — 26 chars
   Rationale: breakup story with an active event — the ex still holds the keys.
   Clear, but needs the reader to picture admin access; longer than #1.
3. `ILeftHimButKeptTheWiFi` — 22 chars
   Rationale: the rare breakup win — she left, the Wi-Fi stayed. Funny, but the
   joke is a shrug of victory rather than a twist; less story than #1.
4. `MomUnpluggedItMidGame` — 21 chars
   Rationale: every gamer's childhood in four words — mom, the plug, the ranked
   match lost. Strong story, but gamer-narrow; strangers outside gaming get
   less of the sting.
5. `TheDogAteTheRouterAgain` — 23 chars
   Rationale: character (dog) + event (ate the router) + "Again" implies a
   history. Cute, but the dog-ate-my-homework trope is well worn.
6. `HoneyTheRouterIsOnFire` — 22 chars
   Rationale: domestic-calm opening, disaster punchline. Funny image, but no
   real story — who set it on fire, why? Criterion 3 is thin.
7. `RoommateMovedOutTookWiFi` — 24 chars
   Rationale: flatmate leaves and takes the internet with them. Same lane as
   #1/#3, but "took WiFi" is flatter than a divorce — less at stake, less funny.
8. `TheWiFiDiedWithMyMarriage` — 25 chars
   Rationale: same marriage lane as #1, but a one-off event ("died") where #1's
   "Since" implies ongoing misery. Longer, weaker punchline.
9. `PasswordIsYourExsBirthday` — 25 chars
   Rationale: a password hint that doubles as a confession — you never changed
   it. A gag more than a story; the event happened long ago and off-screen.
10. `WiFiDownBlameTheToddler` — 23 chars
    Rationale: a household blame game — the toddler did it. Situation + suspect,
    but the "event" is only implied, and toddler-blame is a familiar bit.
11. `FBI Van Moved Out Yesterday` — 27 chars
    Rationale: turns the stock "FBI Surveillance Van" joke into something new —
    the watchers left. But "moved out" is a shrug: who moved, why, what now?
    Criterion 3 is the weakest of the top half.
12. `MyCatSitsOnTheRouter` — 20 chars
    Rationale: every cat owner's truth — warm router, cat on top. Character and
    situation, but no event and no twist; a description, not a story.
13. `DadChangedPasswordAgain` — 23 chars
    Rationale: the household password churn. Relatable, but "again" carries the
    whole joke and the event is mundane — no real story arc.

## Manual ranking (brief criteria in order)

**Winner: #1 `NoWiFiSinceTheDivorce` (21 chars).**

- Criterion 1: a Wi-Fi name, a joke, hints at a story — yes, on its face.
- Criterion 2: the punchline lands on the first read — "Since The Divorce"
  reframes "No WiFi" from a technical fault into a life event. No explanation
  needed; any stranger gets it.
- Criterion 3: the fullest story of the list — situation (no Wi-Fi, still),
  character (the person left behind), event (the divorce, and everything that
  went with it). The reader fills in the rest: the ex, the flat, the silence.
- Criterion 4: fresh — the divorce-as-Wi-Fi-outage lane is not a stock joke.
  The field will flood "Pretty Fly for a Wi-Fi", "It Burns When IP", "FBI
  Surveillance Van" and password puns; none of those tells a story.
- Criterion 5: 21 characters, four clean words, sayable out loud in one breath.

**Pairwise vs the strongest rivals:**

- vs #2 `MyExChangedTheWiFiPassword` (same breakup lane): #1 wins on 2 and 5 —
  its premise IS the punchline, five characters shorter, and it needs no
  admin-access setup. #2's event is sharper, but #1's ongoing "Since" is
  funnier and sadder at once.
- vs #8 `TheWiFiDiedWithMyMarriage` (same marriage lane): #1 wins on 2 and 5 —
  "Since" implies the misery continues; "Died" is a one-off. Four fewer chars.
- vs #4 `MomUnpluggedItMidGame` (strongest non-breakup story): #1 wins on 2 —
  the divorce twist is universal, the gamer twist needs the audience to have
  lost a ranked match to a parent. Tied on 3 and 5.
- vs #11 `FBI Van Moved Out Yesterday` (the stock-joke-turned-new lane): #1
  wins on 3 by a wide margin — #11 names no character and no real event, just
  a van leaving. Turning a stock joke is worth something, but a whole story
  beats a twist on a punchline everyone already knows.
- vs #6 `HoneyTheRouterIsOnFire` (strongest image): #1 wins on 3 — #6 is a
  snapshot, #1 is a before-and-after. A fire is a moment; a divorce is a story.
- vs the stock field ("Pretty Fly for a Wi-Fi" — 23 chars, "It Burns When IP",
  "FBI Surveillance Van"): #1 wins on 3 outright — the stock jokes are
  punchlines with no character and no event. They lose to any Wi-Fi name that
  actually tells a story, which is exactly what criterion 1 rewards.

Runner-up: #2 `MyExChangedTheWiFiPassword` — keep as the standby only; this
entry is ONE submit, no v2.
